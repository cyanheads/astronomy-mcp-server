# 0.4.2 — 2026-10-15

## Added

- **`astronomy_find_events` `body2`** — a `conjunction` of two bodies (the Moon or a planet with another), found where they share an ecliptic longitude seen from Earth's center. Events carry `body2` and `separation_degrees`; `invalid_body_pair` rejects `body2` with another event, the same body twice, or a `sun`/`earth` `body`. (#51)
- **`astronomy_get_rise_set` `night_in_progress`** — for the Sun, a `start` between sunset and sunrise returns the night already under way (dusk through dawn, times before `start` included), since the first cycle then opens at the next sunrise. (#49)
- **`astronomy_get_satellite_passes` visible stretch** — each pass carries `visible_start_utc`/`visible_end_utc` with `_local`, altitude, and azimuth fields. (#47)
- **Typed Horizons refusals** — `ambiguous_designation` lists the candidate ID numbers of a name matching several major bodies, `observer_is_target` rejects Earth, `invalid_elevation` rejects observer elevations Horizons refuses, and `request_rejected` names the input of a request Horizons refuses with HTTP 400. `invalid_step` also covers a step longer than the target's data and a count past 2147483647. (#53)

## Changed

- **Per-call caps** — `astronomy_get_rise_set` `count` accepts up to 366 (31 cycles per call), `astronomy_find_events` `count` up to 200 (20 per call), and `astronomy_get_satellite_passes` `days` up to 30 (10 per call). A capped call returns a notice naming the exact `start` and `count`/`days` that continue the series, or saying no call can when the continuation falls past 2100 or beyond the element set's epoch horizon. Eclipse continuations resume 10 days past the last event. (#50)
- **`astronomy_get_satellite_passes` pass rule** — a pass is kept when its visible stretch (above the horizon, sunlit, Sun below −6°) reaches 10° elevation, wherever in the pass that falls; `sunlit` still reports the peak instant. Rise, set, and visible-stretch edges are bisected to 1 s. (#47, #52)
- **`astronomy_get_rise_set` twilight** — each cycle's pairs are bounded to their own night, so a depth the night never reaches is `null`. (#56)
- **`astronomy_get_rise_set` polar days** — a day with no rise or set (polar night, midnight sun) is a cycle of its own, so consecutive cycles are consecutive days and each night appears in exactly one. (#60)
- **`astronomy_get_ephemeris` request shape** — one request asks Horizons for one page, with `STOP_TIME` clamped to the 201st row, rather than the whole span. (#48)
- **`astronomy_get_ephemeris` dates** — `start` and `stop` are Gregorian (extended before 1582) in years 0100 through 9999; fractional seconds in `start` are kept; `stop` equal to `start` returns the single row at that instant. (#55, #57, #58)
- **`time_out_of_range` recovery** — a year-span rejection from any tool carries that tool's declared recovery on the wire; the satellite tool's declared hint covers both its year-span and epoch-distance checks. (#61)
- **Docs** — README, `docs/design.md`, `CLAUDE.md`, and `AGENTS.md` describe the changes above; the `astronomy_stargazing_plan` prompt reads `night_in_progress` when the sun query starts after sunset.

## Fixed

- **`astronomy_get_ephemeris` calendar-step resume** — a `mo` or `y` step with a count above 1 resumes on the row Horizons prints next, where it previously skipped rows (`5mo` from Jan 31 prints Jul 31). (#54)
- **`astronomy_get_ephemeris` apostrophes and non-ASCII designations** — a designation with an apostrophe (`6P/d'Arrest`) or a character outside printable ASCII (`Šteins`) fails as `body_not_found` naming the record-resolving form (`DES=6P;CAP`, `2867;`), where Horizons searched the text before the apostrophe. (#59)
